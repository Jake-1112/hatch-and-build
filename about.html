About

One person, no code, a lot of trial and error.

Hi — I'm the person behind Hatch & Build. I'm 29, and I'm studying theology, not computer science. I don't work in tech, I've never held a developer job, and there's no team or funding behind anything on this site. It's just me, a laptop, and ideas I couldn't stop thinking about.

A few years ago that combination would have meant the ideas stayed ideas. Today it doesn't. I build everything here with the help of AI tools — describing what I want, testing what comes back, fixing what breaks, and slowly learning how the pieces fit together. I still wouldn't call myself a developer. But the things I build are real, they're live, and people use them.

That's what the egg is about. From egg to ___ — every project starts as something small and unformed, and the fun part is watching what it hatches into.

What I've built so far

  • Drawing Playground — a free real-time drawing wall for parties, weddings, and events. Guests scan a QR code and their drawings float onto the big screen instantly. No app, no sign-up. The first version was built in three days and used live in front of a real audience.
  • GuGu — a group challenge iOS app that helps friends build habits together: pick a challenge, invite your group, and keep each other accountable day by day.
  • DANA (단아) — a Korean-American hair salon platform for the U.S., helping Korean-Americans discover and book trusted salons wherever they live.
  • Hangeulmaru (한글마루) — an all-in-one hub for Korean school teachers: lesson planning, classroom management, and support, in one place.

Why build in public

I share the process — the wins and the embarrassing bugs — on Instagram and on the build log here. Partly because writing things down is how I actually learn them, and partly because I know there are a lot of people like me: full of ideas, convinced they're disqualified because they can't code. I used to be one of them. If watching a theology student ship apps makes one of those people start building, this site has done its job.
